PaperRaven

Generating topic-bounded, verified samples of the scientific literature

Give it your topic and a few papers you trust. It returns a corpus checked against the scholarly databases, judged against criteria you approve, and documented paper by paper.

You bring a topic and a few papers.

The topic, in the researcher's own words:

Whale body size evolution. Literature on their size distribution, and all the evolutionary, metabolic, physiological, and life history constraints and patterns that relate to explaining why whales have the sizes they have. I'm particularly interested in papers with empirical data on whale sizes, which could be paleontological in nature. Taxonomic descriptions are out. Review papers are in.

PaperRaven turns them into criteria you approve.

It reads the seeds, asks where the boundary falls using real example papers, and writes down the answers. Four of them:

Inclusion 4 yours

A description of a new fossil cetacean is in scope only if body size is a substantive result: an estimate with a stated method, or a discussion of the taxon's size within cetacean size evolution.

Inclusion 5 written by the tool

A study of living whales is in scope if it reports body length or mass across individuals, growth curves, or size at age or maturity.

Exclusion 1 yours

Taxonomic descriptions are out.

A ruling on a named paper yours

Bianucci et al. 2023 (Nature), which names Perucetus colossus and estimates its body mass, is in: a taxonomic description that reports a size estimate is in scope.

In all, 8 criteria and 6 judging notes: 13 of the 14 are the researcher's own, and 1 was written by the tool. The export marks which is which.

The LLM proposes; the code checks every paper.

What you can rely on, and what it cannot promise.

Enforced by the code, not the LLM

  • Every paper exists: matched to OpenAlex or Crossref.
  • The details are the database's, not the LLM's memory.
  • Every decision is recorded, with who made it.
  • Nothing disappears silently: every proposal is accounted for.
  • Year limits are rules, not a judge's reading.
  • The export can be checked, file by file.

Not promised

  • Completeness. No tool that asks a model for papers can say how many it missed.
  • Identical relevance calls. Relevance is a model's judgement.
  • The same corpus twice. A second run finds a similar corpus, not the same one.

Safe to run, and polite to the sites it uses.

enforced by PaperRaven's code or by Claude Code · instructed a rule the LLM is told to follow

The LLM is fenced in

  • Only the code writes the run's state; the LLM writes answer files. instructed
  • The judge can only read, write and search the web; the close-out reviewer can only read. enforced
  • Titles and abstracts are data, never instructions. instructed
  • No made-up DOIs, no downloads outside fetch, and never a look at your .env. instructed

Polite to publishers

  • At most one request every 10 s to a publisher, and at most 25 per publisher. enforced
  • It stops asking a publisher at its first refusal. enforced
  • It bypasses no paywall and logs in nowhere. enforced
  • It refuses local and private network addresses, so a link cannot send it into your own network. enforced

Your information stays yours

  • Your email goes only to OpenAlex and Crossref, and is redacted in the export. enforced
  • Your OpenAlex key is never written into a run or its export. enforced
  • No telemetry: nothing goes to PaperRaven's author or anyone else. enforced
  • PDFs never go where git would track them. enforced
